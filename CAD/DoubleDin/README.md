# Godwit double-DIN enclosure

A generic ISO 7736 double-DIN head-unit enclosure. It is built around the
UC-595 7" HDMI panel, the Godwit 4-channel amplifier board and a single-board
computer. It is sized for a Raspberry Pi 5, and Radxa and Rockchip boards fit
the same trays.

`GodwitDoubleDin.FCStd` is generated by `build_double_din.py`. Every dimension
is a cell in the `Design` spreadsheet. Change a cell, recompute, and the model
follows. Exports are in `../exports/Godwit_DD_*.step` / `.stl`.

## Coordinates

- X runs across the unit, centred on the DIN axis. +X is the panel's connector side.
- Z is up, centred on the DIN axis.
- Y runs into the dash. Y = 0 is the bezel front face. The chassis rear is at Y = 172.

## Mounting system

Each tray has rows of X-running M3 slots every 10 mm along Y. Two cases:

- **Hole spacing along Y is a multiple of 10 mm.** Standoffs go straight into
  the slots. The amplifier's 80 mm spacing works this way, at X ±61.3 and
  Y 31 / 111.
- **Any other board.** Put it on two Rails. A rail bolts across two rows
  100 mm apart with M3 socket heads into nuts under the tray, so it slides
  along X. An M2.5 male–female standoff slides anywhere along the rail's
  90 mm slot, held by a nut in the rail's underside channel. Two rails take
  any rectangular four-hole pattern up to about 100 × 90 mm. For the Pi 5's
  58 × 49 pattern, the rails sit at X = −76.5 and −18.5.

Modules without holes, such as the USB hub or sound card, zip-tie through the
slots.

## Rear connectors

| Connector | Use |
|---|---|
| DT04-6P | Power: BATT ×2, GND ×2, ACC, spare. Its pins are rated 13 A, so the amplifier's feed is doubled. |
| DTM04-08PA | Four speakers, + and − each. |
| DTM04-6P | CAN: H, L and signal GND for each of the two buses, so isolated transceivers keep their own reference. |
| 2 × USB-C bulkhead | The two dials (USB HID). |
| 3.5 mm panel jack (M6) | Cabin mic into the USB sound card. The Pi 5 has no audio input. |
| 3 × SMA bulkhead | GNSS, Wi-Fi/BT, FM/SDR. |

The housings come from the TrailCurrent FreeCAD Tools workbench. Each one is
a feature you can edit by double-clicking it, and its X position is bound to
the sheet. Glue the receptacle into its housing; the plug's latch does the
locking. Line-level audio to the amplifier stays inside the case, wired to its
JST-XH inputs.

## Hardware

- Bezel to chassis: 6 × M3 heat-set inserts (Ø4 × 6 holes) and 6 × M3 × 8 screws.
- Panel ears: 4 × M2.5 self-tapping screws into Ø2.2 pilots.
- Side brackets: M5 heat-set inserts in whichever of the 24 pads your bracket uses.
- Rear plate: 4 × M3 self-tapping screws into the corner posts.
- Amplifier: 4 × M3 × 6 standoffs, with M3 screws from under the lower tray.
- Each rail: 2 × M3 × 10 socket heads and nuts. Each board standoff: an M2.5 × 6
  male–female standoff with a nut in the rail channel.

## Check before printing

- **USB-C bulkhead hole.** `usbc_d` is a placeholder (16.2 mm). Set it to the
  part you buy. A flanged two-screw bulkhead needs a different cut-out.
- **Panel connector clearance.** The UC-595's USB-C/HDMI connectors face +X.
  A right-angle adapter has to turn its cable rearward inside the chassis
  wall, 6 mm past the panel edge. `disp_dx` moves the panel.
- **Upper deck height.** The Pi 5's board bottom is at Z = 15, and its tallest
  port tops out at Z 32.5. The inside of the lid is at Z 47.5. A standard HAT
  on a 2×20 stacking header, with screw terminals, reaches about Z 40. Check
  that budget against any HAT you use.
- **Amplifier heights.** These carry the photogrammetry uncertainty in
  `../AmplifierBoard/MEASUREMENTS.md`. There is 7 mm between the heatsink top
  and the upper tray.
- **DT6 housing STL.** The STL is not watertight (one self-intersection in the
  workbench's DT6P housing BREP). Slicers repair it on import, and the STEP
  is a valid solid.
